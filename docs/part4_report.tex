\begin{abstract}
We derive the effective spectrum of fixed diagonal preconditioning for rotated
positive-definite quadratic objectives and verify the result numerically.
We then analyze the group's saved historical main-study data: 10,200 evaluation
runs and 6,600 pairs covering five methods, two learning-rate selection regimes,
and separate spectral-information references. All evaluation runs attained the
target at least once, but standard Adam retained it in only 1,572 of 1,700 runs.
We distinguish historical attainment from final retention, quantify paired
fixed-budget effects, and use structure-preserving bootstrap intervals.
All statements about measured performance refer to the supplied historical
execution, not a newly rerun main experiment. Independent artifact checks pass,
while the recorded sampled numerical replays do not fully reproduce the
historical trajectories.
\end{abstract}

\section{P5: exact diagonal preconditioning}
Let
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c&b\end{pmatrix}\succ0,\quad
 P=\operatorname{diag}(a,b)^{-1},\quad r=\frac{|c|}{\sqrt{ab}}.
\]
Positive definiteness gives $a,b>0$ and $ab-c^2>0$, hence $0\le r<1$.
The symmetric effective Hessian is
\[
 S=P^{1/2}HP^{1/2}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&c/\sqrt{ab}\\c/\sqrt{ab}&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $1-r$ and $1+r$, so
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1+r}{1-r}. \label{eq:p5}
\end{equation}
The relevant quantity is the spectral condition number of $S$, not the
singular-value condition number of the generally nonsymmetric matrix $PH$.
For $H=Q_\theta\operatorname{diag}(\mu,L)Q_\theta^T$,
\[
 a=\mu\cos^2\theta+L\sin^2\theta,\quad
 b=\mu\sin^2\theta+L\cos^2\theta,\quad
 c=(\mu-L)\sin\theta\cos\theta.
\]
At $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, $r=0$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$;
at $45^\circ$, $r=(L-\mu)/(L+\mu)$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=L/\mu$.
Thus fixed eigenvalues permit complete removal of anisotropy or no improvement.

For $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta PHw_t$, use $y=P^{-1/2}w$ to obtain
$y_{t+1}=(I-\eta S)y_t$. The stable interval for all initial points is
$0<\eta<2/(1+r)$. The minimax constant step is $\eta_*=1$,
with worst-direction contraction $r$ in the Euclidean norm of $y$, equivalently
the $P^{-1}$-norm of $w$, and objective bound
$f(w_t)/f(w_0)\le r^{2t}$. This is an explicit-Hessian, fixed-preconditioner
comparison, not Adam's time-varying normalization and not a practical oracle-free claim.

\section{Independent numerical validation}
We sweep all integer angles from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ at
$\kappa\in\{10,100,1000,10000\}$. All 364 comparisons of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p5} with a symmetric eigensolver pass; maximum relative error is
$8.32\times10^{-13}$. Endpoints and the transformed exact recurrence are tested.
An additional 56 runs use seven angles, four spectra, and GD/diagonal GD,
each with 2,000 gradient calls. The same canonical direction is used across
angles within a spectrum. Diagonal GD uses $\eta=1$; GD uses its theoretical
spectral step. These one-direction checks are analytical references, not equal-budget
tuned estimates or additional held-out replicates. Axis-aligned diagonal GD solves
the quadratic in one update up to roundoff, whereas a $45^\circ$ rotation removes
its condition-number advantage.

\section{Data audit and statistical protocol}
The saved main protocol contains 5,760 pilot, 52,000 tuning and 10,200 evaluation
runs. Evaluation uses 50,000 gradient calls with checkpoints at 1,000 and 10,000;
selection uses 16 candidate rates and a 5,000-call tuning horizon. Transfer and
orientation-specific retuning are analyzed separately. GD/heavy-ball spectral
references form a separate \texttt{theory} regime, not extra equal-budget tuned runs.
The two-dimensional study has ten held-out directions at each spectrum,
and the $p=20$ study has ten held-out rotations with five paired directions each.
Condition number one is retained as an isotropic control.

Runs are joined by explicit reference IDs, with matching algorithm, regime,
spectrum and initialization seed. All 6,600 references resolve uniquely.
All saved fixed-budget paired effects exactly match independent recomputation
from the evaluation CSV in this environment. Integration review matched the
contributor's original archive SHA256 to its Release asset digest; a newly
assembled team archive has its own identity. Historical raw results and
metadata remain unchanged.

For a checkpoint $B$, define
\[
 r_B=f(w_B)/f(w_0),\qquad
 \Delta_B=\log_{10}\max(r_{B,Q},10^{-16})-
           \log_{10}\max(r_{B,I},10^{-16}).
\]
Positive effects mean a larger rotated objective gap, not a universal ranking
between methods. Both observations must be finite at $B$; a last finite iterate
from an earlier failure is never substituted. We report medians, quartiles,
missing counts and pointwise descriptive percentile 95\% intervals from 2,000
bootstrap draws (seed 502004). Two-dimensional directions are jointly resampled
across all angles. Higher-dimensional resampling selects rotation clusters and
retains all initialization pairs in a selected cluster. Draws are shared across
methods/regimes for a spectrum; reused references are not counted as new
independent observations. There are only ten independent resampling units per
cell. Intervals are not simultaneous bands or multiplicity-adjusted tests.

Historical success means first attainment $T=\min\{t:r_t\le10^{-6}\}$ exists;
final retention means the completed run still satisfies the target at the final
checkpoint. Both definitions have complete four-way paired tables. Attainment
ratios $T_Q/T_I$ use only pairs with both first times available, and disclose that
subset size. Missing times are not filled with the budget. Failed or unexecuted
runs remain in denominators. Fixed-budget log-gap summaries use all available
finite observations and disclose exclusions; empty bootstrap draws are omitted
only when a requested metric is undefined for every selected unit.

\section{Results on the saved historical execution}
\subsection{Attainment, retention and failures}
All 10,200 evaluation runs are marked \texttt{finite\_cap}: they used the full
budget rather than stopping at first attainment. All attained the target at least
once. This status does not mean the target is retained. Standard Adam retains
the target in 777/850 transfer runs (91.41\%) and 795/850 retuned runs (93.53\%);
the pooled count is 1,572/1,700 (92.47\%). All other saved methods retain the
target in every evaluation run. These pooled percentages are descriptive,
not independent-binomial inference across the heterogeneous design.

\subsection{Orientation effects depend on budget and tuning}
At $p=20$, $\kappa=10^4$, standard Adam's transfer median $\Delta_{1000}$ is
12.17 (descriptive 95\% interval [12.05,12.28]); after retuning it is
$-2.08$ ([$-2.49$,$-1.90$]). At 50,000 calls the respective medians are
0.29 ([$-0.21$,1.14]) and 2.96 ([1.52,3.87]). The sign and size depend on
the checkpoint and selection regime. These values do not establish a persistent
orientation ordering, a convergence rate, or significance after broad exploration.
The $10^{-16}$ floor limits apparent precision when both gaps are very small.

\section{Reproducibility, costs and limitations}
The contributor's archived independent verification checks complete saved plans,
selections, CSV fields and pairing before numerical sampling. Its structure
checks pass. With ten reruns per phase (30 total), two eigenbasis-Adam runs exceed
$\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-8}$, $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-12}$ on nine compared fields.
Therefore \textbf{the contributor's numerical replay does not fully pass}.
The prior integration audit separately reports 56 mismatched runs among 450
sampled reruns; that is a separate check, not the contributor's 30-run sample. Neither
number denotes the count of divergent evaluation runs. The saved historical
\texttt{verification.json} is preserved, not promoted to a current all-clear.
The cause of every historical numerical discrepancy is not established here.
New unit tests and deterministic statistical recomputation passing do not resolve
the long-horizon optimizer replay mismatch.

The experiment exposes the full Hessian. Diagonal extraction requires $O(p)$
work/storage and elementwise preconditioning $O(p)$ per step, beyond a dense
$O(p^2)$ gradient. The inherited runner extracts the diagonal inside its update;
its zero reported setup time is not evidence of zero setup cost. Multiworker
historical run times include CPU contention and are not controlled speed
benchmarks. Dedicated setup-inclusive timing was a secondary extension in the
Proposal and is not claimed as completed here. No runtime ranking is inferred.

This report concerns deterministic quadratics, a small number of independent
directions/rotations, a finite candidate grid and unequal tuning/evaluation horizons.
Late Adam observations are numerically sensitive. Bootstrap intervals describe
the stored observations conditional on that execution; they do not incorporate
platform uncertainty. Any stronger numerical-reproducibility claim requires a
new frozen, explicitly identified execution or a resolved explanation agreed by
the group, not silent replacement of selected historical rows.
